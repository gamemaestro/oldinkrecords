Octavia E. Butler: The Grandmother of Solarpunk

She died two years before the word solarpunk appeared. Her stories planted the seeds.

Octavia E. Butler was an American science fiction writer born in Pasadena, California, in 1947. She never used the word solarpunk. At Old Ink Records, we call her the grandmother of the movement anyway, because so much of what solarpunk believes was already alive in her work.

Her life

Butler was born on June 22, 1947, and grew up in Pasadena. She wrote from a young age and built her career in a genre that rarely made room for writers like her, becoming one of the most celebrated science fiction authors of her generation. She died on February 24, 2006, near Seattle, Washington.

The Parable novels

In Parable of the Sower (1993) and Parable of the Talents (1998), Butler imagined a near-future California unraveling under environmental and social crisis. At the center is a young Black woman who refuses to give up. She plants seeds, gathers people, and builds a community that insists on survival and meaning even while the world burns around them.

Readers have returned to these books again and again. In 2020, nearly three decades after it was first published, Parable of the Sower reached the New York Times bestseller list.

Her honors

Butler won two Hugo Awards and two Nebula Awards, two of the highest honors in science fiction, including the Nebula for Parable of the Talents. In 1995 she became the first science fiction writer to receive a MacArthur Fellowship.

Why we call her the grandmother of solarpunk

The word solarpunk first appeared online in 2008, two years after her death. Its spirit was already in her pages: people choosing to build community, to grow food, and to imagine a future worth surviving for, even when everything around them says it is impossible. That is the solarpunk spirit in its most essential form.

Solarpunk’s vision was born in California, and Butler is a big part of why. Her stories taught generations of readers to imagine what could go right, and they still light the way. Read our guide to solarpunk.

Thank you, Octavia.
