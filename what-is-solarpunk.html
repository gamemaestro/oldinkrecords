What Is Solarpunk?

Rejecting dystopia. Choosing optimism. A plain-language guide to the movement, where the word came from, and what it believes.

Solarpunk is a creative movement in fiction, art, design, and everyday life that imagines a sustainable, just, and beautiful future and asks people to start building it now.

For a long time, the future we were shown was mostly a warning: collapsing cities, surveillance, machines turned against us. Solarpunk answers with a different question. What does a future worth living in actually look like, in detail, and what can we do today to move toward it?

What the word means

The name blends two ideas. Solar stands for renewable energy and care for the living world. Punk stands for a do-it-yourself, grassroots spirit that refuses the story that collapse is inevitable. Put together, solarpunk means choosing optimism on purpose and then getting to work.

Where solarpunk came from

The earliest known use of the word is a 2008 blog post titled “From Steampunk to Solarpunk,” written about a cargo ship that used a giant computer-controlled kite to save fuel. From there the idea grew quickly:

  • 2012: The first published anthology of solarpunk fiction, Solarpunk: Histórias Ecológicas e Fantásticas em um Mundo Sustentável, edited by Gerson Lodi-Ribeiro, appears in Brazil.
  • 2014: On Tumblr, the artist Miss Olivia Louise suggests pairing solarpunk with an Art Nouveau look. Her drawings of green, sunlit cities spread widely and give the movement its signature style.
  • 2018: Ellery Studio holds the Solar Punk Festival in Berlin, bringing artists, scientists, and builders together around real-world solutions.
  • 2022: Becky Chambers’ hopeful novella A Psalm for the Wild-Built wins the Hugo Award for Best Novella, bringing solarpunk to a wide new audience.

Long before the word existed, writers born or raised in California were already imagining these futures. Octavia E. Butler wrote from Pasadena. Ursula K. Le Guin was born in Berkeley. Kim Stanley Robinson grew up in Southern California. Their books are often read as solarpunk’s intellectual ancestors.

What solarpunk believes

The future is not fixed. The stories we tell about tomorrow shape what we build. Imagining only disaster makes disaster feel like the only option.

Technology and nature belong together. Solar panels beside food gardens, cities designed for walking and cycling, tools built to serve people and the planet.

Change grows from the ground up. Community gardens, repair culture, local makers, and neighbors sharing what they know.

Imagination is a practical tool. Every artist, writer, and dreamer who makes a better world feel tangible is helping to build it.

Solarpunk vs. cyberpunk

Cyberpunk is often summed up as “high tech, low life”: dazzling technology in a broken, unequal world. Solarpunk keeps the technology and changes who it serves. Its cities are bright, green, and built around people, and its heroes are gardeners, engineers, artists, and neighbors.

Solarpunk worldbuilding

Worldbuilding is the practice of imagining a world in enough detail that you could walk around in it: how people get their food and energy, what their homes look like, what they celebrate. Solarpunk worldbuilding turns that practice toward a future worth wanting. It is where many solarpunk ideas are tested first, in stories, art, and games, before they show up in the real world.
