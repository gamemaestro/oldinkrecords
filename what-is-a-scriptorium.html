What Is a Scriptorium?

The room where the record is kept, and why Old Ink Records calls itself a solarpunk scriptorium.

A scriptorium (plural: scriptoria) is a room for writing, where scribes copied, decorated, and preserved books by hand. The word comes from the Latin scribere, “to write.”

The definition

Merriam-Webster defines a scriptorium as “a copying room for scribes especially in a medieval monastery.” Britannica describes it as a writing room set aside for the scribes who copied manuscripts. It is pronounced skrip-TOR-ee-um.

A short history

Through the Middle Ages, every book in Europe was made by hand. In scriptoria, scribes copied texts letter by letter onto parchment, and illuminators decorated the pages with color and gold. The work was slow and exacting, and it is a large part of why so many older writings survived to reach us.

Not every scribe lived inside the walls. Britannica notes that lay scribes and illuminators from outside also worked alongside them, and over time the craft spread to workshops in growing towns. The printing press, which arrived in Europe in the mid-1400s, gradually took over the work of copying, and the age of the scriptorium came to a close.

What a scriptorium means today

The tools have changed, and the idea has stayed powerful: a place devoted to keeping the record, so that what people learned, made, and dreamed is not lost. Anyone who keeps a journal, saves family stories, or documents their work is part of that lineage.
